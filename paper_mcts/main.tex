\begin{abstract}
Designing competitive agents for 8-ball pool requires balancing aggressive offensive play with robust defensive strategy under stochastic physical dynamics. This paper details the development of \texttt{NewAgentPro}, an agent driven by an adversarial Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) framework. We address the limitations of traditional heuristic-based search in continuous action spaces through the implementation of progressive widening and stratified sampling. Furthermore, we introduce an opponent-aware reward function with an adaptive weighting scheme (the ``Lambda Ladder'') to balance potting ambition with defensive caution. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed agent achieves a competitive win rate of \MCTSWinVsPro\ against the high-performance \texttt{BasicAgentPro} baseline, while maintaining low catastrophic foul rates through a multi-stage verification mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}

Autonomous decision-making in billiards presents a unique intersection of continuous control, stochastic physics, and adversarial planning. While heuristic-based agents can excel at immediate potting, they often lack the long-term strategic depth required to defeat opponents that employ lookahead search, such as those using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS).

This project focuses on the development of \texttt{NewAgentPro}, an agent specifically designed to counter high-level MCTS opponents. The core of our approach is the transition from a pure heuristic evaluation to a deep search-based architecture. However, applying MCTS to 8-ball pool is non-trivial due to the high-dimensional continuous action space and the scarcity of rewards. Our primary contributions include a stratified sampling technique to ensure diverse action coverage, an opponent-aware reward structure for strategic play, and a noise-alignment procedure to bridge the gap between simulation and execution.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}

We consider the 8-ball pool game as a stochastic Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a continuous action space $\mathcal{A} \subset \mathbb{R}^5$, representing shot parameters $(V_0, \phi, \theta, a, b)$. Each action is subject to Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \Sigma)$. The agent's goal is to maximize the probability of winning, which involves not only pocketing own-group balls but also mitigating opponent threats.

We define an adversarial reward function at each state $s$:
\begin{equation}
R(s) = R_{own}(s) - \lambda \cdot \text{max\_threat}_{opp}(s)
\end{equation}
where $R_{own}(s)$ is the immediate potting reward, and $\text{max\_threat}_{opp}(s)$ is a heuristic estimate of the opponent's best possible response. The parameter $\lambda$ controls the defensive sensitivity.

\section{METHODOLOGY}

\subsection{MCTS with Progressive Widening}
To handle the infinite branching factor in the continuous action space, we employ Progressive Widening. The number of children $k$ of a node $v$ is limited as a function of its visit count $n(v)$:
\begin{equation}
k(v) = \lfloor C_{pw} \cdot n(v)^\alpha \rfloor
\end{equation}
where $C_{pw}$ and $\alpha$ are hyperparameters. This ensures that the search gradually explores new actions while refining the evaluation of existing ones.

\subsection{Stratified Action Sampling}
A common failure mode in MCTS for billiards is the concentration of samples on a few nearby balls, missing high-quality shots on distant targets. We implement a stratified sampling scheme to ensure a uniform distribution of candidates across the table.

As shown in Algorithm \ref{alg:stratified}, we group potential actions by their target balls and sample uniformly from each group.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Stratified Action Enumeration}
\label{alg:stratified}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Table state $s$, Candidate count $K$
\Ensure Action set $\mathcal{A}_{cand}$
\State $G \gets \text{GroupActionsByTarget}(s)$
\State $\mathcal{A}_{cand} \gets \emptyset$
\State $i \gets 0$
\While{$|\mathcal{A}_{cand}| < K$}
    \State $target \gets \text{Targets}[i \pmod{|Targets|}]$
    \State $a \gets \text{PopRandomAction}(G[target])$
    \State $\mathcal{A}_{cand} \gets \mathcal{A}_{cand} \cup \{a\}$
    \State $i \gets i + 1$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $\mathcal{A}_{cand}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Opponent-Aware Reward and the Lambda Ladder}
The weight $\lambda$ in Equation 1 is critical for balancing aggression and defense. A fixed $\lambda$ often leads to either excessive risk-taking or paralyzing conservatism. We propose the **Lambda Ladder**, an adaptive scheduling mechanism where $\lambda$ is a function of the remaining balls $N_{rem}$:
\begin{equation}
\lambda(N_{rem}) = 
\begin{cases} 
0.05 & \text{if } N_{rem} > 3 \\
0.25 + 0.1(3 - N_{rem}) & \text{if } N_{rem} \le 3 
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This allows the agent to play aggressively in the early game to clear the table and switch to a defensive posture in the endgame.

\section{IMPLEMENTATION CHALLENGES}

\subsection{Simulation-Reality Noise Alignment}
We observed a significant performance gap between MCTS simulations and the actual environment. Analysis revealed that the internal noise model used for search did not match the environment's physics. By explicitly synchronizing the standard deviations $\sigma_{\phi}=0.1$ and $\sigma_{spin}=0.003$, we improved the search accuracy and reduced execution errors.

\subsection{Fatal Foul Mitigation}
In standard rules, failing to hit a rail after ball contact is a foul. Initially, our MCTS underestimated the impact of this rule. We elevated the \texttt{FOUL\_NO\_RAIL} penalty to a catastrophic level ($R = -\infty$ in simulations), effectively forcing the search to prioritize legal contact and rail contact.

\subsection{Double Verification Mechanism}
To mitigate the impact of lucky samples in MCTS rollouts, we introduce a verification stage for the best-performing action.
\begin{equation}
\text{ActionSafe} = \frac{1}{N_{test}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{test}} \mathbb{I}[foul_i = 0] > 0.98
\end{equation}
If the optimal action fails this $N_{test}=40$ sample verification, the agent falls back to the next best action.

\section{ALGORITHM EVOLUTION}

The development of \texttt{NewAgentPro} proceeded through four distinct milestones:

\textbf{Milestone 1: Heuristic Baseline.} Used Bayesian optimization for parameter tuning but lacked adversarial depth.
\textbf{Milestone 2: Pure MCTS.} Introduced the tree search framework but suffered from high variance and poor candidate coverage.
\textbf{Milestone 3: Opponent Modeling.} Added defensive rewards. Initially led to over-conservatism until the Lambda Ladder was introduced.
\textbf{Milestone 4: Final System.} Integrated stratified sampling and the verification mechanism, achieving a stable \MCTSWinVsPro\ win rate.

\section{CONCLUSION}

The development of \texttt{NewAgentPro} highlights the importance of domain-specific structural priors in Monte Carlo Tree Search. By combining stratified sampling with an adaptive adversarial reward function, we successfully navigated the trade-off between offensive power and defensive safety. Future work will explore the use of learned value functions to replace hand-crafted heuristics in the MCTS expansion.
